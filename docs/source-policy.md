# Evidence and editorial review

Prefer exact technical support matrices, configuration guides and release notes over general marketing statements for hard support decisions. Use official data sheets for hardware tables and ordering identifiers, and licensing/ordering text for commercial rules. Published family capabilities are not a complete PID/firmware/license/deployment support matrix. Label those mappings as derived and conditional until technical prerequisites are established.

Compatibility states are supported, unsupported, conditional, unknown. Absence from a list and a dash without an explicit legend do not establish unsupported. Unknown never passes a hard requirement. Isolated supported features do not prove supported coexistence. A current public data sheet does not establish current sales lifecycle.

Record the original URL, title, vendor date if known, UTC check date, exact section/table and a short useful excerpt. Do not fabricate document anchors. Store hashes/excerpts instead of entire vendor documents. Preserve TLS and checksum validation. Fetch public documents only; honor robots.txt, rate limits, terms and copyright. No scraping behind authentication or confidential/internal Cisco sources.

Every initial approved assertion in this repository identifies Codex as its initial source reviewer and explicitly retains publisher review. This is not a completed independent human sign-off. Before a public release, the publisher should inspect the cited excerpts and restrictions. Subsequent compatibility, licensing and battlecard changes require review through a pull request; automation cannot approve or deploy them. Preserve and surface conflicts/superseded sources instead of silently selecting a favorable result. Treat community and editorial material as distinct from published vendor evidence.

These limit the scope of published claims; they are not hidden by a passing data validator.
